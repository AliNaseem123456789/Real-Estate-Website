# How property is sized and priced in Pakistan

## Units
- 1 **marla** = 225 square feet in most housing societies. Some older revenue records in
  parts of Punjab use 272 square feet per marla, so check which one a seller means.
- 1 **kanal** = 20 marla = 4,500 square feet.
- 1 **square yard** = 9 square feet. Karachi plots are often quoted in square yards.
- Common plot sizes: 5 marla, 10 marla, 1 kanal, 2 kanal. In Karachi: 120, 240 and 500
  square yards.

## How prices are quoted
- 1 **lakh** = 100,000 rupees. 1 **crore** = 10,000,000 rupees = 100 lakh.
- Sale prices are usually spoken in lakh or crore: "85 lakh", "1.6 crore".
- Rent is spoken in thousands per month: "45k", "1.2 lakh per month".
- **Price per marla** is how buyers compare plots within an area, because it strips out
  the effect of plot size. Comparing two plots by total price alone is misleading if they
  are different sizes.

## Judging whether a price is fair
- Compare against the median price per marla for the same neighbourhood, the same
  purpose (sale or rent) and the same category (house, flat, plot, commercial). An
  apartment and a plot in the same area are not comparable.
- Within a neighbourhood, a corner plot, a wider road, a park-facing position or a plot
  near the main boulevard carries a premium. A plot near a graveyard, a grid station or
  a nullah sells at a discount.
- Compare the size band too. The price per marla of a 5 marla plot is usually higher than
  that of a 1 kanal plot in the same area, because smaller plots have more buyers.
- **Rent-to-price ratio** is the quick investment test: how many years of rent equal the
  purchase price. A lower number means a better rental yield.
- Sample size matters. A median built from four listings tells you very little; treat it
  as a hint, not a valuation.
