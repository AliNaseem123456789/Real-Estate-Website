# What to check before buying a property in Pakistan

## Documents to ask the seller for
- **Title document.** For land held in the revenue record this is the *fard malkiat*
  (record of rights) plus the *intiqal* (mutation) history. For a housing society or an
  authority scheme it is the allotment letter and the transfer letter in the current
  owner's name.
- **Approved building plan**, stamped by the relevant development authority, for any
  built property. A house built outside its approved plan can be a problem later.
- **Completion or occupancy certificate**, where the society issues one.
- **No Objection Certificate (NOC)** from the society or authority confirming the plot
  is transferable and has no outstanding dues.
- **Non-encumbrance certificate** confirming the property is not mortgaged or under a
  court order. In Punjab this can be checked at the Arazi Record Centre.
- **Paid utility bills and society dues**, most recent ones, in the seller's name.
- **CNIC copies** of every owner on the title, and a registered power of attorney if
  someone is signing on an owner's behalf.

## Checks worth doing yourself
- Verify the title at the land record office or the society office in person. Do not
  rely on photocopies the seller provides.
- Confirm the society or scheme itself is approved by the relevant development authority.
  Unapproved schemes are the most common way buyers lose money.
- Match the plot number, dimensions and location on the documents against the physical
  plot. Have the plot demarcated by the society if there is any doubt.
- Check whether the land is under any acquisition notice or planned road widening.
- For agricultural land being sold as residential, confirm the land-use conversion has
  actually been done.

## Red flags
- The seller will only show photocopies, or hurries the buyer past verification.
- The price is far below the going rate for the area for no stated reason.
- The title is in the name of a deceased person and succession has not been completed.
- Several people claim a share and not all of them are present or represented.
- The society has no approval, or the file is a "file" with no plot allotted yet.

Property law and the documents required vary between provinces and between societies.
Treat this as a checklist of what to look for, and have the final set of documents
reviewed by a property lawyer before paying anything beyond a token.
