# Transfer process and the costs around a sale

## How a transfer usually runs
1. **Agreement to sell** with a token or earnest payment, listing the price, the timeline
   and what happens if either side backs out.
2. **Verification period**, during which the buyer checks the title, the NOC and the dues.
3. **Society or authority transfer**, or registration of the sale deed at the registrar's
   office for land in the revenue record. Both parties usually need to be present with
   CNICs and photographs.
4. **Mutation (intiqal)** entered in the revenue record, so the buyer's name appears in
   the *fard*. A transfer is not finished until this is done.
5. **Possession handed over**, along with original documents and the clearance of all
   utility and society bills.

## Costs a buyer should budget for, beyond the price
- **Stamp duty** and **registration fee** on the sale deed, set by the province.
- **Capital Value Tax (CVT)** where the province charges it.
- **Federal withholding tax on the purchase** under the Income Tax Ordinance, charged at
  a different rate for filers and non-filers. The seller pays a separate withholding tax
  on the sale.
- **Society transfer fee**, which societies set themselves and which can be a fixed
  amount per marla.
- **Agent commission**, commonly around 1% from each side, negotiable.
- **Lawyer or deed writer fee** for drafting and vetting.

Rates for all of these change with each federal budget and vary by province, by whether
the buyer is a tax filer, and by the property's declared value. Always confirm the
current figures with the registrar's office, the society and a tax practitioner before
budgeting. Being on the FBR active taxpayer list before the transaction usually reduces
the withholding rate noticeably.

## Renting instead
A rental agreement should be written, registered with the rent registrar where the
province requires it, and should state the rent, the increase schedule, the security
deposit, the notice period and who pays which utility. One or two months' rent as
security deposit and an annual increase stated as a percentage are the common pattern.
